Installation is straightforward but has a hard requirement: this requires a solid mounting surface. The included screws work for wood studs, concrete, brick, and masonry. The listing explicitly warns against plasterboard and drywall unless there's a wooden stud behind it. That's not a vague disclaimer — a bike hanging vertically puts significant leverage on the mount. A 30-pound bike at the end of a 3-foot lever arm generates far more force at the wall than the bike's static weight. If you can't find a stud or drill into masonry, this product won't work for your space. The 3-year warranty covers defects, and the two-pack pricing means you can store two bikes in the same wall section, which is practical for households with multiple riders. The swivel aspect becomes more valuable with two mounts — you can angle each bike to overlap slightly, reducing the total wall footprint. Limitations & Data Gaps The listing doesn't specify the mount's own weight or dimensions beyond tire compatibility. That makes it hard to estimate how far the mount protrudes from the wall when not in use, or whether the arm length accommodates different wheel diameters. A 29er with a large-diameter wheel might position the bike differently against the wall than a 26-inch wheel, and the listing doesn't clarify the hook arm's reach. In real-world use, the fender compatibility claim is mentioned but not detailed — fenders add width and can interfere with hook designs, so if you run full fenders, this is worth verifying before buy. Summary This is a functional, space-saving solution for garages or sheds with solid walls. The 150-degree rotation and 77-pound capacity handle most standard bikes, and the two-pack covers multiple bikes efficiently. The rubber coating protects wheels, and the bearing system suggests smooth operation. The main limitation is installation surface — you need a stud or masonry, not drywall. If your mounting surface is solid and your tires are under 90mm wide, this is a practical choice. If you have a fat bike or only drywall available, look elsewhere. Who this is for If you have two bikes and a garage wall with exposed studs or concrete, this mount solves a real problem. The swivel lets you park bikes at angles that fit around cars or shelves. Commuters with road bikes and weekend riders with standard mountain bikes will find the capacity and tire width limits enough. Homeowners with masonry walls in basements or sheds will appreciate the included hardware matching their surface. Who should look elsewhere If you ride a fat bike with tires over 3.5 inches wide, this won't fit. If your garage uses drywall without accessible studs, the mount won't have a secure anchor point — no amount of hardware fixes an unsupported wall. Renters who can't drill into concrete or studs should also pass.
