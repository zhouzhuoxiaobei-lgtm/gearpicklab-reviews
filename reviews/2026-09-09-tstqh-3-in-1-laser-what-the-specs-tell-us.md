For most people, the accuracy claims need a caveat: ±2mm is the laser spec, and ±1/16 inch is for the tape. Those are different tools with different tolerances, and the listing correctly separates them. The Pythagorean mode (calculating heights via indirect measurement) is useful for ceiling heights you can't reach, but it compounds error - the further you measure, the less precise the result becomes. Summary The TSTQH 3-in-1 Laser combines a 164-foot laser distance meter, a 1-meter tape, and a dual-axis level into one rechargeable unit. It suits indoor DIY work - room dimensions, furniture layout, shelf alignment - where ±2mm accuracy and the three reference points handle most tasks. The [USB-C](https://www.usb.org/usb-type-c) charging and 50-set history are practical conveniences. On the practical side, the lack of published weight and dimensions makes it hard to judge portability, and the tape-plus-laser form factor will be bulkier than a dedicated laser measure. Who this is for This makes sense if you do regular home projects and want one tool for measuring, marking, and leveling. An apartment dweller hanging shelves, a renter laying out a recent sofa, or a homeowner installing baseboards would all find the combined functions useful. The 1-meter tape handles the close work, the laser covers the room, and the angle sensor checks that your shelf brackets are level. The 164-foot range is overkill for most interiors, but the entry-level model keeps the size down. Who should look elsewhere If you need a dedicated laser measure for job-site work - something that survives drops, has a published IP rating, and comes with a hard case - this isn't that. For comparison, the combination tool trades some ruggedness and precision for versatility. Similarly, if you already own a decent tape measure and a separate laser level, the TSTQH doesn't replace either with equal quality. It's a convenience tool, not a professional instrument.
