we noticed the 300 CFM figure is the number to sit with. For light duty - boiling pasta, searing a steak on a weeknight, reheating leftovers - it moves enough air to pull smoke and grease away from the cooktop. For high-output cooking, it won't keep up. If you regularly run a wok or deep-fry, this class of hood will leave you with lingering smoke and a greasy film on the cabinets. The general rule of thumb is that a hood should move roughly 100 CFM per 10,000 BTUs of burner output; a standard gas range with four 9,000-BTU burners puts out 36,000 BTUs and wants closer to 360 CFM. This unit falls short of that. For everyday listening. The 4.7-inch height is the real selling point. Under-cabinet hoods in this slim category typically run five to six inches deep. So this sits at the low end. In an apartment or a kitchen with low upper cabinets. That extra inch of headroom over the counter matters more than the suction number. The 55 dB noise rating at top speed is plausible for a two-speed fan this size, but treat it as a manufacturer figure rather than a measured one. That said, two speeds is also a genuine limitation - most hoods at this spec level offer three. You get low and high, nothing between, so simmering something delicate means either full fan noise or none. In real-world use, the filter situation is worth flagging. The listing describes the filter as both a "3-layer aluminum filter" in the title and a "5-layer composite filter" in the features, which is contradictory. Filter replacement is recommended every 3 to 4 months, and the box contents list only "Filter" - no mention of a charcoal filter for ductless operation. If you plan to run this recirculating rather than vented. Confirm what filter media is included before you commit, because ductless setups need carbon filtration to do anything about odors. What the specs don't tell you There's no stated motor wattage. No duct diameter, and no mounting hardware list. Duct diameter matters if you're replacing an existing hood and want to reuse the wall or cabinet cutout - standard is 6-inch round or 3.25-by-10-inch rectangular, and neither is specified here. There's also no warranty information in the provided data. If those details matter to your install, they're worth confirming before buy rather than after. Summary The Soonye hood fits a narrow use case well: space-saving kitchens, apartments, and remodels where vertical clearance under the cabinets is the binding constraint. The 300 CFM fan and two-speed toggle are adequate for light cooking, and the 4.7-inch height is among the slimmest in this category. Buyers who cook heavily, or who need a ductless setup with proven odor control, should look at higher-CFM units with three or more speeds and clearly specified carbon filtration. This is a functional, low-profile vent hood for modest cooking, not a workhorse. Who this is for This suits renters and compact-space owners who need a 30-inch hood that tucks under standard cabinets without eating counter space. Think a studio apartment with an electric coil stove. Or a basement kitchenette where the ceiling is low and the cooking is mostly eggs and pasta. The toggle switch and two speeds are also easier to operate than touch panels if you want something with no learning curve. Who should look elsewhere If you have a gas range with high-output burners. Cook stir-fries or fried food regularly, or need a ductless installation with real odor control, this unit's 300 CFM ceiling and underspecified filtration will leave you frustrated.
