The unit has 5 hooks made from what the description calls "kitsite" - presumably a typo for kirsite, a zinc-alloy family. For everyday listening. The body is solid wood. What the specs mean in practice
Five hooks is a reasonable count for a single household's daily carry - one per person's keys plus a spare or two. The listing says the hooks handle "heavy stuffs like overcoat, large backpack," which is a broader claim than most key racks make. Kirsite is a real zinc-alloy family used in hardware, and it's stronger than stamped steel sheet hooks at similar thickness. But without a stated weight limit, that claim is the manufacturer's word, not a documented figure. If you're hanging a wet winter coat every day. You'd want that number and it isn't here. One more point: the pre-drilled template is the most concrete aspect. It means you don't freehand the spacing, and the listing notes it prevents "riddled wall" from mis-drilled holes. For renters or anyone hanging this on drywall, that's a real convenience - one template, three or four holes, done. In real-world use, the solid wood construction is a step up from the MDF or plastic you'll find at this tier of wall organizers. Solid wood takes stain and paint better and generally resists the stripping you'd see around screw holes in particle board. The listing describes the finish as "rustic" and "retro," which is decorative language, not a spec. What you can rely on is the material claim itself: solid wood. Summary

If you're mounting on brick. Tile, or plaster, you'll need your own hardware and the listing won't help you choose it. The "5 minutes" installation claim is a time estimate.
