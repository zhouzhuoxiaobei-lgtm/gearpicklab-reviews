 ### Features to Consider
 ### Material and weight capacity
 The product names give you the material in some cases — Notmise and Jusgym are metal. Metal generally handles more weight and holds up better outdoors than particleboard, but it also dents and scratches visibly. The Jusgym 2x4ft panels being metal means they're likely intended as a base, a backing, or a modular frame element rather than a finished surface. If you're expecting a polished furniture look, metal panels may disappoint. If you're building a workbench or a garage assembly. They're a reasonable starting point.

 ### Assembly complexity
 Beginners consistently underestimate assembly. A 26-drawer unit like the IRIS USA model is basically a plastic frame with 26 sliding trays — simple in principle. But tedious to load and label. A 72-inch metal cabinet usually arrives flat and requires two people to stand upright safely. The Decent C Camping table is the opposite: folding furniture is built to set up in under a minute. Which is its entire selling point. If you have no tools and no patience. Prioritize products that arrive assembled or fold flat.

 ### Footprint versus usable space
 This is where the slim storage cart and the kitchen stand earn their place. A slim cart fits in the gap between a refrigerator and a counter, or beside a desk. A kitchen stand typically adds a counter-level surface in a room that doesn't have one. Both are space-optimized. By contrast, the 26-drawer unit and the 72-inch cabinet demand floor space and reward you with volume, not convenience. Decide which you're short on: floor area or storage capacity.

 ### Mobility
 Only some of these products move easily. Slim storage carts usually roll. Which makes them useful for cleaning and reconfiguring. Metal cabinets and drawer units typically don't. And once loaded, they're effectively permanent. Camping furniture folds but isn't meant for daily indoor use. If you rearrange your space often, weight and wheels matter more than you'd think.

 ### Durability expectations
 None of these products are heirloom furniture. Plastic drawer units crack at the corners under repeated stress. Thin metal panels flex. Folding camping tables wear at the hinge points. That's fine if you're buying for a first apartment. A dorm, a garage, or a temporary setup. It's not fine if you want something to last a decade. Set your expectation based on how you'll use it. And be honest about whether you'll actually take care of it.
