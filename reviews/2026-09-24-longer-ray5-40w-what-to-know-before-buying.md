Title: Longer RAY5 40W - A Class 4 Laser Cutter With 24,000 mm/min Speed and a Safety Rating That Demands Respect If you're weighing a 40W diode laser for a garage workshop, the Longer RAY5 40W sits in a bracket where raw cutting numbers matter more than polish. You want to know whether it can cut the material in front of you, how fast it moves through a job - and what the safety classification actually means for how you work. This unit answers two of those questions clearly and leaves the third open. The headline figure is a single-pass cut of 30mm black acrylic, 20mm wood, and 0.15mm stainless steel. Those are aggressive numbers for a diode-class machine. And they define what kind of shop this belongs in. The 0.08 × 0.1mm focus spot and 256-color capability on stainless steel point to detail work as well as bulk cutting. For everyday listening, the catch is the Class 4 rating, which is the highest laser hazard class and means the beam can injure eyes and skin even from scattered reflection. If you're engraving colored images onto stainless steel, the air assist keeps the oxide layer consistent, which is what produces the color variation. Without it, color results get muddy. In real-world use. The 3.5" touchscreen and offline app control matter for workflow. You can run jobs from the panel or over the network. And the spec sheet claims one device can control multiple machines. That's a production characteristic, not a hobby attribute. If you're running two or three units in a small shop, central control changes how you schedule work. The 24/7 continuous operation claim is also a production spec, and it's worth reading as a ceiling rather than a routine. Continuous running at that duty cycle depends on the cooling and motion hardware holding up, and the data doesn't describe the cooling system beyond a temperature protection cutoff. Safety hardware is where this machine tries to cover its Class 4 rating. Motion detection, temperature protection, and an emergency stop switch are listed, along with safety goggles. The goggles are a starting point, not a full solution, for a Class 4 beam. A laser at this class can damage eyes from direct exposure and from reflected light off metal surfaces, so enclosure or a controlled workspace with proper eyewear rated for the wavelength is the realistic configuration. The auto-shutdown on abnormal movement or overheating is useful. But it's a backstop, not a substitute for a safe room. Limitations and data gaps The spec sheet is thin on several things a buyer calls for. There's no listed working area. So you can't tell how large a piece this machine can hold. No weight. No frame dimensions, no power draw, and no cooling method are given. Those gaps matter because a 40W laser generates real heat and a Class 4 machine demands a defined footprint. The 24/7 continuous operation claim is also unqualified. No duty cycle. No thermal derating curve, and no ambient temperature range appear in the data, so treat that number as a marketing ceiling rather than a verified spec. The offline app control is described as a aspect but the data doesn't say which platforms it compatible with or whether it works over Wi-Fi or Bluetooth. If you're planning to run jobs remotely, that's a question the listing doesn't answer. The multi-machine control attribute is similarly unqualified: no limit on how many units, no description of the software layer that coordinates them. Who this is for This machine suits someone with an established workspace who needs to cut thick acrylic and wood in volume and wants engraving speed that keeps up. A compact signage shop cutting 20mm wood panels and 30mm black acrylic would use the single-pass capability daily. A metal fabricator engraving colored designs onto stainless steel would use the 256-color palette and fine focus spot. Both are people who already understand Class 4 laser safety and have the space and eyewear to match. Who should look elsewhere If you're buying your first laser and planning to run it on a kitchen table. The Class 4 rating makes this the wrong starting point. The safety requirements alone, proper eyewear, a controlled area, and probably an enclosure, add cost and complexity that a lower-class machine avoids. If your work is small-scale and occasional, the 40W power and 24,000 mm/min speed are overkill, and the missing working-area spec makes it hard to know if your typical project even fits. And if you need verified duty-cycle data before committing to continuous production, this listing doesn't provide it. Summary The Longer RAY5 40W is a high-power diode laser built for cutting thick acrylic and wood and engraving colored detail onto stainless steel. Its 40W module, 24,000 mm/min speed, and 0.08 × 0.1mm focus spot make it a production-oriented tool, not a hobbyist starter unit. For most people, the Class 4 rating and the missing working-area and cooling specs are the two things to resolve before buying.
