 The material spec is the first thing worth flagging. 201 stainless steel is a lower grade than the 304 you'll find on better pitchers. For everyday listening, it's still steel and it handles milk fine, but 201 has less nickel and more manganese, which makes it more prone to surface staining and pitting over time, especially if you leave milk residue in it or run it through a dishwasher repeatedly. Hand wash and dry it, and it'll hold up. Treat it like a workhorse and it'll show wear faster than a 304 pitcher would. The 600mL capacity is the practical middle of the range. That's enough for frothing milk for one large latte or two smaller drinks in a single pass. Which saves you the annoyance of splitting batches. The 250mL and 350mL sizes are tight for anything beyond a single cortado. In real-world use, the 1000mL and up are for batch frothing or space-saving cafe use, and at 680g for the largest size, you'll feel the weight in your wrist after a few pours. Non-drip spout and a lid both matter more than they sound. The spout is what lets you control the pour for latte art - a bad spout dribbles and ruins your surface. The lid keeps milk from sloshing when you're moving from counter to machine. And it traps heat between pours. For office use. Where you're carrying the pitcher from a shared kitchen to a desk, the lid earns its place. Limitations and data gaps The listing doesn't give you wall thickness or bottom diameter. And those two numbers matter for frothing. A thinner wall heats faster but gives you less control over microfoam texture. Without them, you're buying blind on the most tactile part of the job. The 1-3cm measurement tolerance the listing admits to is also wide for a vessel this miniature - a 1cm variance on the 600mL version could mean the difference between a user-friendly pour and a cramped one. There's no word on whether the lid seals tightly or just rests on top. "Spill-proof" in the feature copy is doing a lot of work without a gasket spec to back it up. Assume it's splash-resistant, not leak-proof. Summary This is a low-cost entry into home milk frothing with a sensible 600mL size. A lid, and a pour spout that covers the basics. The 201 stainless steel is the main compromise - it's serviceable but not the grade you'd want for daily heavy use or dishwasher cycles. It suits someone frothing once a day for one or two drinks, not a household running a pitcher through constant wear. Who this is for You're making one or two lattes a day at home or at a desk. You want a lid so you're not cleaning up spills, and you're fine hand-washing a pitcher. If you're learning latte art and need a entry-level vessel to practice pour control, the 600mL size gives you enough milk to make mistakes without wasting a whole carton. Who should look elsewhere If you're frothing multiple drinks back to back, running the pitcher through a dishwasher, or you want the durability of 304 stainless, this isn't the one.
