in our experience the bannetons use cotton rope rather than rattan. Rattan baskets have a reputation for shedding splinters and holding onto moisture between bakes; cotton rope wicks moisture away from the dough surface. Which is what you want for a dry exterior that releases cleanly and browns well in the oven. No sharp edges is a real difference if you have kids in the kitchen or you're tired of picking fibers out of your dough. The tradeoff is that the cotton rope proofing baskets generally need a thorough flour dusting and careful drying. And the listing itself warns against sudden temperature changes when cleaning the jar - glass under a hot tap straight from a cold shelf is the usual way these break. The 34-ounce starter jar is the right size for maintaining a single active starter without it climbing the walls after a feed. Wide-mouth matters more than capacity here: narrow jars make it hard to get a spatula in and near-impossible to clean dried starter off the shoulder. Measurement lines and a tracking tool are included, which saves you guessing at a 1:1:1 feed ratio. Two lames with eight replacement blades is generous. A curved blade handles the classic crescent score; the straight blade is for a single clean slash or a cross pattern. Blades dull rapid on high-hydration dough, so eight spares covers a lot of loaves before you need to buy more. The two silicone slings are the other underrated inclusion - they let you lift a proofed loaf out of the basket and lower it into a hot Dutch oven without deflating it. For comparison.
