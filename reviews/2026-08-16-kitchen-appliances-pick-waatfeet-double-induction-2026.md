What the specs mean in practice For boiling water or making coffee, this unit works fine — just expect it to take a bit longer than a dedicated single-burner cooktop when you're using both sides. If you're making a no-frills meal with a pot on one burner and a pan on the other, the shared 1800W means each side runs at roughly half power. That's fine for simmering and warming, but not ideal for searing or stir-frying at high heat. The independent controls are the saving grace. You can run one side at P10 and the other at P2 without fiddling with shared settings. The button interface is uncomplicated: press to select your level, and the display shows P1 through P10 clearly. The compatibility check is worth noting. Induction cooktops need ferromagnetic cookware — if a magnet doesn't stick to the bottom of your pan, it won't work. Summary The waatfeet Double Induction Cooktop suits anyone who requires two cooking surfaces in a portable footprint and doesn't need full power on both burners at once.
