 The material spec is the one worth anchoring on. 304 stainless is the standard food-grade alloy for this kind of kitchen tool - it resists rust and staining. And it doesn't react with milk. The listing also calls it 18/8 stainless. Which is the same alloy under a different naming convention (18% chromium, 8% nickel). No discrepancy there. The internal scale is the feature that separates this from a plain stainless jug. It's marked in ounces and milliliters. And the listing claims the markings won't scrape off. If you're steaming for one person, you want roughly 4-6 oz of milk in a 12oz pitcher; for two drinks, closer to 8-10 oz. Having the scale printed inside means you stop guessing and stop over-pouring. Which is the most common cause of wasted milk at home. Note that the listing says "milliamps" in one place where it clearly means milliliters - that's a typo in the copy, not a real unit. The point-mouth spout is the other functional spec. A narrow. Sharp lip is what lets you control the pour rate at the end of a latte art attempt. A wide or rounded spout dumps milk in a sheet and ruins the pattern. For everyday listening. This one is described as dripless, which is the claim you'd want to test first, since a pitcher that drips from the spout base is worse than useless when you're trying to draw a line. The handle is heat-resistant and the base is concave. The concave base matters more than it sounds - a flat-bottomed pitcher can rock on a steam wand or a countertop, and a pitcher that shifts while you're pouring at a 30-degree angle will cost you the pattern. The 304 steel is also dishwasher-secure, which is worth knowing if you make multiple drinks a day. Where the data runs thin There's no weight listed. For a 20oz pitcher that's a real omission, because a heavy pitcher is harder to control during a slow pour. Most stainless pitchers in this size land somewhere between 6 and 10 ounces empty; without the figure, you're guessing. There's also no wall thickness spec, which is the number that tells you how fast the pitcher heats through and whether the steel feels flimsy or substantial in hand. Thicker walls hold temperature more steadily but take longer to heat on the steam wand. The latte art pen is included but the listing gives no detail on it - no material, no tip size, no dimensions. Treat it as a bonus rather than a reason to buy.
