Title: PREXISO Laser Level — Green Beam Brightness With a Self-Leveling Catch If you’re laying floor tile across a 20-foot room, a laser level that drifts or blinks mid-run is worse than no laser at all. The PREXISO Laser Level claims a 100-foot green beam range and self-leveling within ±4°. But the fine print matters more than the headline numbers. The unit runs on a 2600mAh rechargeable battery via Type-C. Which is standard for this class of tool. The magnetic pivoting base rotates 360° and mounts on a 1/4"-20 thread, so it will attach to most tripods or metal studs. You get three projection modes—horizontal. Vertical, and cross—from two laser modules. Most entry-level self-leveling lasers offer ±3° to ±4°, so this sits at the typical end. If your floor or wall is tilted beyond 4°, the laser blinks and the lock LED turns red—it won’t pretend to level itself. That’s honest behavior, but it means you need a reasonably flat surface or a tripod to get started. The manual mode locks the pendulum for angled lines, which is useful for stair stringers or sloped trim, though the listing doesn’t specify how accurate that locked mode is. The 100-foot range is optimistic. For everyday listening, the manufacturer notes that strong sunlight weakens laser energy and recommends indoor use. On a sunny job site, expect that range to shrink dramatically. The included target plate helps in bright rooms—it reflects the green beam back, making the line easier to spot—but it’s not a substitute for shade. This is an indoor-first tool that can occasionally work outdoors under overcast skies or in shaded areas. Build-wise, the TPE soft rubber cover adds shock resistance and grip. The listing claims dustproof, wear-resistant, and cold/high-temperature tolerance, but there’s no IP rating provided. That’s a gap. Without an IP number, you don’t know if it survives a light rain or just a dusty shelf. For a tool marketed for construction, the missing ingress protection spec is worth noting. Summary The PREXISO Laser Level suits indoor trades like tile setting, cabinet installation, and wallpaper hanging, where the green beam’s visibility and the magnetic base make quick work of aligning fixtures. Its ±4° self-leveling range and 100-foot claim are typical for this price tier, but the lack of an IP rating and the explicit indoor-use note limit its outdoor credibility. If your work stays inside and you need a rechargeable cross-line laser with a solid mounting system, this fits. If you need a rugged outdoor tool or require a wider self-leveling range, keep looking. Who this is for: a tile installer working in finished rooms, a carpenter hanging cabinets or doors, or a DIYer renovating a basement. The magnetic base sticks to metal door frames or conduit, and the 360° pivot lets you aim the line without re-leveling. The Type-C charging means you can top it up from a laptop charger on site. Who should look elsewhere: anyone working outdoors regularly, or who demands a laser that tolerates dust and moisture without a second thought. The missing IP rating and the manufacturer’s own sunlight warning make this a poor pick for exterior framing or deck work. If you need a plumb-dot laser or a rotating laser for grading, this isn’t that tool either—it only projects lines, not points.
