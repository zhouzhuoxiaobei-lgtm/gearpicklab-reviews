what caught our attention the 0.03-inch barrel thickness is worth pausing on. That's thin stainless. It will conduct heat efficiently. Which helps with the 2-to-3-hour distillation window, but it also means you should treat the boiler with some care during handling and cleaning. A thicker wall would hold heat more steadily and resist dents better. But it would also add weight and measured the heat-up. This is a trade-off, not a flaw, but you should know which side of it you're on. Cooling is handled by a copper coil with a 0.39-inch diameter running through a 0.92-gallon condenser keg. Copper conducts heat well. And the surface area of that coil is what determines how quickly vapor condenses back into liquid. A 0.92-gallon condenser is on the smaller side for a 50-liter boiler. So the included water pump matters. It circulates coolant through the condenser continuously. Which keeps the temperature gradient stable. Without it. You'd be changing water constantly. The pump plugs in and detaches for cleaning, which is a practical detail - condenser residue is sticky and unpleasant to deal with if the pump isn't removable. Sealing is handled by four buckles and silicone gaskets inside the lid. Plus food-grade silicone tubing. The tubing is described as flexible and resistant to kinking, which matters because a kinked tube on a still is a pressure problem, not just an inconvenience. There's also a one-way exhaust valve for fermentation. That valve lets gas escape without letting air back in. Which is standard for fermentation but worth noting because it means the unit can serve double duty as a fermenter before you ever apply heat. For comparison, in real-world use, the thickened bottom accepts gas stoves, firewood, and electric ceramic heaters. That's a broader range of heat sources than you'd get from a unit with a thin, flat base. If you already have a propane burner or a wood stove, you can use it. If you don't, you'll need to factor in the cost of one, because the still doesn't include a heat source. What's missing from the data is as telling as what's included. There's no stated maximum operating temperature. No pressure rating, and no warranty information. For most people, the materials are listed as food-grade, which covers the silicone, stainless steel, and copper, but there's no third-party certification mentioned. For a product where you're boiling alcohol vapor, those are gaps worth acknowledging. The 2-to-3-hour distillation period is also a single figure with no context - it doesn't specify the starting temperature of the wash or the ambient conditions, so treat it as a rough guide rather than a guarantee.
