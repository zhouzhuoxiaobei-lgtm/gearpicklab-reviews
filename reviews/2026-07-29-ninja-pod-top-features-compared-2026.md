# Ninja Pod: Top Features Compared (2026)

Title: Ninja Pod and Grounds Specialty Single Serve Coffee Maker — Dual brewing with pods and grounds, built-in frother, compact footprint If you’re trying to decide between a pod machine for quick mornings and a ground coffee brewer for better flavor, this Ninja model removes the choice. It handles both in one unit, and adds a milk frother for lattes and cappuccinos. The question is whether its capabilities match how you actually drink coffee. The specs show that pB051ST (Stone finish) is a single-serve machine that covers many serving sizes. For pod users, you get the standard 6, 8, 10, or 12 ounces. For grounds, the range extends from 6 ounces all the way to 24 ounces — enough for a large travel mug. There’s no carafe option; every brew is made one cup at a time. The built-in frother folds away when not used and is removable for cleaning. A 56-ounce reservoir gives you several brews before refilling. The “Specialty” mode brews a concentrated coffee that you then mix with frothed milk for drinks like lattes and macchiatos. That’s the main reason to choose a machine with a frother built in — you can make milk-based drinks without a separate appliance. The “Over Ice” style brews stronger, so melting ice doesn’t leave you with watery coffee. “Rich” and “Classic” adjust extraction time for intensity. All four are available whether you use pods or grounds. The grounds brew range up to 24 ounces is unusual for a single-serve unit. Most pod machines cap at 12 or 14 ounces. If you fill a large travel mug, you can brew directly into it using the grounds basket — no need to run two cycles. The pod side is standard: just pop in a K-Cup and select your size. The 56-ounce reservoir is removable, which makes filling and cleaning easier than fixed-tank machines. That capacity gives roughly 5 standard cups or 2 large travel mugs before a refill. No data on brew time or temperature, which is a gap. Most similar machines take around a minute for a pod brew; grounds may take slightly longer, but without specs, that’s an assumption. Limitations & data gaps The biggest omission is physical dimensions. Ninja calls this “portable” and mentions a storage drawer, but without width, depth, or height numbers, you’ll need to measure your counter space yourself. Similarly, there’s no power rating (watts), no brew temperature, and no information on auto-off or energy saving. If those matter to you, look for a spec sheet from the manufacturer. The frother is a simple whisk-style attachment, not a steam wand. It creates frothed milk by spinning — adequate for lattes and cappuccinos, but won’t produce the microfoam of a dedicated espresso machine. Looking at the data, it’s removable and dishwasher fail-safe, which is capable for maintenance. Also note: this is a single-serve brewer. You cannot make a full pot of coffee. Each cup is individual, and using grounds requires you to scoop and dispose of the basket each time. Pods are more convenient but create more waste. Summary This Ninja Pod and Grounds machine suits someone who wants the option of both pod speed and ground coffee quality, plus the ability to make milk-based drinks, all in a single unit that takes up less counter space than separate devices. What the specifications tell us is that the large 24-ounce grounds brew capacity covers travel mugs, and the 56-ounce reservoir means fewer refills. Missing specs on dimensions and brew time mean you’ll want to verify fit and speed yourself, but the core aspect set — dual brewing, four styles, built-in frother — is well-defined for this price tier. Who this is for You have a mix of morning habits: some days you grab a K-Cup and go, other days you want to use fresh grounds for better flavor. You also occasionally want a latte or cappuccino without buying espresso gear. The 24-ounce grounds option means one brew fills a large insulated mug. If that describes your routine, this machine consolidates three appliances (pod brewer, drip brewer, milk frother) into one compact unit. Who should look elsewhere You primarily drink regular black coffee and don’t need milk frothing — a simpler pod- or grounds-only machine will suffice. If you serve multiple people at once (family, office) or want a full pot, this single-serve unit isn’t for you. Those who demand precise temperature control or steam wand microfoam (for latte art) should look at higher-end espresso machines.
