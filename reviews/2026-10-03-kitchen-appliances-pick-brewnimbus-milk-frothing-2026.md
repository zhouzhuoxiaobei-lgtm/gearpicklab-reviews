what stood out to me in our experience the 600ml figure is total volume, not working volume. BrewNimbus itself says to pour 200-300ml, which leaves headroom for the steam wand to inject air without overflowing. That's a normal ratio for this class of pitcher, and it maps cleanly to 8-12oz cups. If you drink 16oz* (based on available data) lattes, you'll be steaming twice. If you make two 8oz cappuccinos back to back, one fill covers both. The 304 stainless steel body is the standard food-grade alloy for milk pitchers. It conducts heat evenly, which is why steam wand users can feel the temperature through the metal and cut the steam at the right moment. The Teflon coating is the more interesting choice. For everyday listening, it makes cleanup faster, but Teflon is a soft surface. Metal spoons and abrasive scrubbers will scratch it, and BrewNimbus explicitly warns against abrasive scrubbers. Hand-wash with warm water and mild detergent, or run it through the dishwasher, and the coating should hold up. Treat it like a non-stick pan and it won't. The handle is plastic. That's a deliberate trade: plastic insulates better than a bare metal loop, so your hand stays user-friendly when the pitcher is full of hot milk. That said, in real-world use, the catch is the 60°C ceiling. BrewNimbus says the pitcher can be briefly heated on a stove at low temperature but must not exceed 60°C. That's not a suggestion; it's the melting point of the handle. So while the base is induction-compatible, this pitcher isn't a stovetop milk warmer. It's a steam-wand pitcher that happens to tolerate a quick warm-up. If your workflow involves heating milk directly in a saucepan, this isn't the tool for that. Weight comes in at 210 ± 5 g, light enough to control with one hand during a pour, and the 3.62 × 4.37 in footprint is compact enough to sit beside a home espresso machine without crowding the counter. The wide spout and extended flow channel are the features aimed at latte art, and the included pen gives you a tool for dragging crema after the pour. What's missing from the data is just as worth noting. There's no stated wall thickness, no warranty information, and no temperature rating for the Teflon coating itself. Those gaps don't make the pitcher a poor choice, but they mean you're relying on the 60°C limit and the dishwasher-safe claim as the durability story. For a -class accessory, that's a reasonable set of knowns, but it's not a complete picture. Summary This pitcher suits someone pulling 8-12oz drinks at home who wants a coated, straightforward-clean vessel with a heat-shielded handle and a latte art pen in the box. The 600ml capacity and 200-300ml working volume line up with standard home espresso ratios, and the 304 stainless body is the right material for steam wand work. The 60°C stovetop ceiling and plastic handle mean it isn't built for direct high-heat use, so anyone who warms milk in a pan should look at an uncoated stainless pitcher with a metal handle instead. Who this is for: a home barista who steams 200-300ml of milk per drink, uses an 8-12oz cup, and wants a non-stick interior to cut cleanup time. If you're pulling two cappuccinos every morning and rinsing the pitcher between them, the Teflon surface and dishwasher-safe claim are the practical wins here. Who should look elsewhere: anyone who heats milk directly on a burner, uses a metal whisk or spoon inside the pitcher, or regularly makes drinks larger than 12oz. For most people, the plastic handle and the 60°C limit rule out stovetop workflows, and the coating rules out metal utensils.
