 The V-spout is the spec that actually changes your pour. A narrow V concentrates the milk into a thin stream, which is what you need to drop a heart or drag a rosetta without the flow widening and blobbing the design. If you've been pouring from a wide-lipped jug or a measuring cup, the difference in control is immediate and it's the main reason to buy a dedicated pitcher at all. The etched oz/ml scale solves a smaller but real annoyance: recipe reproducibility. If a cortado calls for a specific milk volume, you can measure it in the pitcher instead of eyeballing a separate cup and dirtying another dish. The markings are etched into the steel rather than printed, which the listing states as permanent - worth noting because printed scales on cheaper pitchers wear off after enough scrubbing. Food-grade stainless steel is the standard material for this class of pitcher. And the listing claims rust and corrosion resistance along with a non-porous surface that rinses clean. That's a reasonable expectation for stainless, but the data doesn't specify the grade (304 vs. 201 matters for long-term corrosion resistance), so treat the material claim as generic until you've handled one. The handle is described as rounded with a heat-resistant grip. Which helps when you're holding a pitcher that's just been under a steam wand - but again, no wall thickness is listed, and thin walls transfer heat to the body faster than thick ones. For everyday listening. The gaps are worth flagging. There's no stated weight, no wall thickness, no dishwasher-protected claim, and no warranty information anywhere in the specs. For comparison, for a -25 category item that's mostly fine, but if you plan to run it through a dishwasher daily, the listing doesn't confirm it's rated for that. Hand washing is the secure assumption. There's also no measurement graduation on the outside, only inside, so you'll be tilting the pitcher to read the scale - normal for this design, but something to know. For someone pulling one or two espresso drinks a day and wanting to move past messy pours, the 350 ml size and narrow spout are the right combination, and the etched scale is a genuine convenience. If you regularly steam for a household of four or batch milk for multiple drinks, step up to the 550 or 900 ml - the 12 oz version will have you refilling mid-service.
