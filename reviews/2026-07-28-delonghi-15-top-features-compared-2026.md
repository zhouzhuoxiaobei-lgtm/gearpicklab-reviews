# De'Longhi 15-Bar Pump Espresso Machine with Milk Frother & Temperature Control — Small Home Barista Coffee Maker for Espresso, Latte, Cappuccino & Iced Coffee, Uncomplicated to Clean Design — Decent Entry-Level Machine with Manual Learning Curve If you've been using a pod machine and want real espresso without doubling your appliance budget, this De'Longhi sits in a familiar spot: a 15-bar pump, a manual steam wand, and a compact footprint. It's the kind of machine that assumes you're willing to learn a few techniques — not the kind that does everything for you with one button. The core promise is straightforward: 15 bars of pressure. which is the standard for home espresso machines. That's enough to extract from finely ground coffee at around 9 bars at the puck — the rest is overhead. Unlike cheaper steam-driven units, this has an actual pump. That matters for getting a decent crema and proper extraction. The specs show that "adjustable controls" mention is vague — there's no PID temperature control or pressure gauge included. What you can adjust is likely the brew dose via the filter basket size and the steam wand flow manually. ## What the Specifications Tell You About Daily Use The "Advanced Cappuccino System" is a manual frother — a steam wand with a panarello sleeve. Panarello wands inject air into the milk stream to create froth. They're easier to master than a traditional wand but produce a wetter, bubble-larger foam than a pro wand. If you prefer microfoam for latte art. this will take practice. Froth quality is uniform but not silky. The three-in-one filter holder accepts a single-shot basket, double-shot basket, and E.S.E. pods. Pods are convenient but limit you to pre-ground. pre-measered doses — you lose the ability to dial in freshness or grind size. The double basket is useful for a standard 14-18g* (based on available data) dose. No pressurized basket is mentioned, which means your grind needs to be fine and consistent to avoid channeling. Looking at the data, water tank is top-access, removable, and has visible level markings. No capacity is given. but typical for this class is 30-40 oz* (based on available data) — enough for 3-4 double shots before refilling. The drip tray is double-layer: the top grate catches drips. the bottom holds overflow. Removable and dishwasher-safe parts (drip tray. water tank) make cleanup easier than fixed-reservoir machines. ## What's Missing From the Specs There's no weight listed. Given the compact. narrow footprint, expect it to be around 8-10 lbs* (based on available data) — light enough to slide under a cabinet but not so heavy it's hard to move. No wattage either. Most 15-bar pump machines draw 900-1100W* (based on available data); you'll want a 15-amp circuit if other appliances share the outlet. No indicator for brew water temperature or stability. Entry-level machines often suffer from temperature fluctuation between shots. You'll need to flush the group head before your first shot and wait 30-60 seconds between pulls to stabilize. The "easy to clean" claim is relative. What the specifications tell us is that the removable parts are dishwasher safeguarded, but the brew group isn't serviceable — scale buildup on the boiler and gaskets will require periodic descaling. No mention of a built-in water filter; hard water will shorten the machine's lifespan. Summary This De'Longhi is a solid entry-level home espresso machine for someone who wants real pressure from a pump, manual control over milk texturing, and the flexibility to use beans or pods. Based on the available information. 15-bar pump and three filter options cover the basics, but the lack of temperature control, pressurized baskets, or auto steam functions means you'll need to put in some practice to get cafe-quality results. If you value low effort over craftsmanship. a super-automatic or pod system will serve you better. ## Who This Machine Suits You're someone who already makes espresso at home and wants to move past a stove-top moka pot or a steam-toy machine. You're willing to buy a decent burr grinder (this machine doesn't have one) and learn dial-in basics: grind fineness, dose, tamp pressure. You make one or two drinks at a time, mostly milk-based — lattes and cappuccinos — and you don't mind the manual steam routine. The portable size means it will fit on a counter with limited depth. ## Who Should Look Elsewhere If you want a machine that grinds. doses, tamps, and steams at the press of a button, skip this. The manual frother and basket system require hands-on work every shot. If you entertain often and need to pull multiple drinks back-to-back, the lack of dual boilers or a thermoblock will cause frustrating wait times for steam recovery. Also, if you're strictly a pod user who doesn't plan to switch to fresh grounds, the pod option is there but limited — there are better pod-specific machines for convenience. This De'Longhi earns its place if you're well-fitting trading convenience for control. The specs show it's a capable starting point, not a shortcut. Image Alt Text: 1.
