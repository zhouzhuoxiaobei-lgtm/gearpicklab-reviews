What the specs mean in practice Class II means the laser is eye-protected under normal use but still bright enough to be visible outdoors in shade. It's the standard for this category. Not a differentiator. What does differentiate the LD70 from entry-level meters is the combination of the Pythagorean indirect mode and the stakeout function. Indirect measurement lets you calculate a height or width you can't physically reach - say, the peak of a gable - by shooting two distances and letting the meter do the trigonometry. Stakeout does the opposite: you enter a spacing interval, and the meter counts down as you move, which is how you'd lay out studs at 16 inches* (based on available data) on center or space fence posts evenly without marking every position first. The continuous angle display is useful in a way that's effortless to overlook. One more point: most distance meters give you a number and nothing else. If you're measuring something at an angle - a roof pitch, a sloped driveway - you need to know how far off horizontal you are, or the distance reading is misleading. Real-time angle output means you can adjust your position until the angle reads what you want, then take the shot. Memory for 20 readings is adequate for a single room or a space-saving layout job.
