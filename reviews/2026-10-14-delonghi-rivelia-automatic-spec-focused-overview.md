That's roughly a half-pound of beans per hopper, which is a normal capacity for a home super automatic - not generous, not stingy. What matters is that you can pull one hopper out, drop the other in, and grind fresh without cross-contamination. If you keep a decaf and a regular on rotation, or a dark roast and a single origin, this eliminates the awkward middle step that most machines in this class make you live with. The 13-setting burr grinder is a narrower range than some super automatics offer. In practice, 13 steps covers the useful territory for espresso through drip-style long coffee, and the Bean Adapt system automates the grind, dose, and temperature settings per bean type - so you're not manually hunting through all 13 positions anyway. The tradeoff is that the if you like to fine-tune grind independent of the machine's recommendations, you have less granularity to work with than a 20-plus step grinder would give you. Eighteen presets covers the standard lineup: latte, cappuccino, cortado, flat white, espresso, iced coffee, espresso over ice. If your drink isn't in the list, the intensity and quantity adjustments plus the extra shot function give you room to approximate it. That's normal for this category. And it's a reasonable set. The LatteCrema Hot system froths and auto-cleans, which is the main convenience selling point on the milk side. Cold foam requires the LatteCrema Cool accessory, and that's sold separately - worth knowing before you assume iced drinks come with cold foam out of the box. The machine does list iced coffee and espresso over ice as presets. So you can make cold drinks, but the cold foam texture is an add-on. Four user profiles is a space-saving number. If you live in a household of two, that's plenty. If you have four or more regular coffee drinkers, some people will be sharing a profile. It's a minor issue but worth flagging. Limitations and data gaps The spec sheet doesn't list weight. Dimensions, water reservoir capacity, or wattage. There's also no warranty information provided, which is common but always worth confirming before buy on a machine with this many moving parts: grinder, milk system, touchscreen, two hoppers. The cold foam accessory being a separate buy is the one place where the feature list reads slightly better than the reality. If cold foam drinks are a priority, factor that in. Summary The Rivelia is a super automatic built around one genuinely useful idea: interchangeable bean hoppers that let you switch coffee types without emptying or purging. The 13-setting burr grinder, 18 presets, and four user profiles are solid but standard for this class of kitchen appliance. For everyday listening, it makes the most sense for a household that regularly drinks two different beans - regular and decaf, or two roasts - and less sense if you only ever brew one. Who this is for This suits a two-person household running two different beans, or a single drinker who wants decaf available without committing the whole machine to it. If you're the kind of person who keeps a dark espresso roast and a lighter filter roast on hand and resents choosing between them, the hopper swap is the whole argument for this machine. The four user profiles also fit a household where two people have very different drink preferences and don't want to re-dial settings every time. Who should look elsewhere If you drink one bean and one drink, you're paying for hopper-swapping you'll never use. And if you want manual control over extraction - pressure profiling, precise grind adjustment, hands-on steaming - a super automatic is the wrong category entirely, this one included.
