 That 15 mg caffeine figure is the number worth pausing on. A standard cup of green tea runs somewhere in the 25-50 mg range depending on leaf and steep. So this sits on the low end - closer to a light cup than a morning substitute for coffee. Drinking the suggested 3-4 cups gets you to roughly 45-60 mg across a morning. Which is a reasonable amount if you want a gentle lift without the crash of a full-strength coffee. The trade-off is that the if you're using tea as your primary caffeine source. This blend won't get you there in one mug. The 3-minute steep at boiling water is a fast, forgiving method. Green tea brewed at a full boil for too long turns bitter. And three minutes is short enough to avoid that if you pull the bag on time. The instruction to use two bags for a stronger cup is a practical workaround if you want more flavor without extending the steep. Though it does mean the 96-bag count drains twice as fast if that becomes your default. On the organic side, the certifications are stated plainly: USDA Organic, Non-GMO, and vegan. Limitations and data gaps The specifications don't list a per-bag weight, so there's no way to judge how strong a single bag is relative to other green teas beyond the steep instructions. One more thing to note: the "kombucha" here's dried kombucha from fermented black tea used as an ingredient, not a live or liquid kombucha. If you're buying this expecting the probiotic content of a bottled kombucha. The data doesn't support that - it's a tea blend with kombucha as a flavoring and ingredient component.
