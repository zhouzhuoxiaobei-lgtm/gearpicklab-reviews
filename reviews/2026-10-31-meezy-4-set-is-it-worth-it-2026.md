The glass weights are the part most worth understanding. At 2.7 inches across, they're sized to drop into a wide-mouth opening and press vegetables below the brine line, which is the whole point of a weight - anything floating above the liquid is exposed to air and can spoil. That said, for everyday listening, the non-porous glass surface is dishwasher protected, so cleanup is straightforward and the weights won't hold onto odors between batches the way porous materials can. The airlocks are 4.8 inches tall and have removable top caps for releasing gas manually. That height matters if you're stacking jars on a shelf with limited vertical clearance - a jar plus lid plus airlock needs roughly five inches of headroom above the rim. The silicone grommets are the seal between the airlock and the lid; food-grade silicone is the right material here, and it's the part that will wear out first with repeated removal and washing. Stainless steel lids address a real problem with fermenting in standard metal canning lids, which can corrode under the acidic conditions of a long ferment. Rust-resistant stainless is the sensible choice for this class of fermentation hardware.
