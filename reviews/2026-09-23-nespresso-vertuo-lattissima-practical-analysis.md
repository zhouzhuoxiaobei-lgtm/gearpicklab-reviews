Title: Nespresso Vertuo Lattissima - Six Brew Sizes and a Milk Tank. With One Capsule Catch You want a cappuccino at 6 a.m. without pulling out a pitcher, a thermometer, and a separate frother. That's the specific case the Vertuo Lattissima is built for. It's a capsule machine that handles the milk step too, and the spec sheet tells you most of what you need to know about whether that trade-off works for your counter. The short version: it brews six sizes, makes hot foam from a 16 fl, oz tank, and heats up in 30 seconds. For everyday listening, the catch is on the capsule side, and it's worth understanding before you commit. The Nespresso Vertuo Lattissima by De'Longhi is a countertop coffee and espresso maker that pairs Vertuo's centrifugal brewing with a detachable milk system. If you already have a milk routine you like, this machine may be redundant. If you don't, it collapses several steps into one. Key specifications

That range covers the two things most people actually drink at home: a large morning cup and a miniature afternoon espresso. The single and double espresso settings are separate from the coffee sizes, so you're not guessing at a ratio. The 30-second heat-up matters more than it sounds. Most mornings you're not waiting on a machine by choice, and half a minute means you can start a pod before you've finished deciding what to wear. In real-world use, the adjustable drip tray is a space-saving thing that becomes obvious the first time you brew a short espresso into a compact cup and don't want it splashing from too far up. The milk tank is the headline capability and the one with the clearest limits. At 16 fl, oz, it's sized for a handful of drinks before a refill, which fits a household of one or two. It detaches and goes in the dishwasher. So cleanup is a rinse-and-load rather than a scrub. One-touch recipes mean the machine handles frothing and dispensing; you're not steaming milk yourself. What the data doesn't tell you is foam texture or temperature, and those are the two things that separate a decent milk system from a mediocre one. That's a genuine gap. Iced brewing is listed as supported. Which is worth noting if you drink cold coffee for half the year. The machine brews hot or over ice depending on the pod, so the same unit covers both seasons without a second appliance. Limitations and data gaps The capsule lock-in is the biggest constraint. This machine takes Nespresso Vertuo capsules exclusively. And that's stated plainly. You can't walk into a grocery store and grab a generic pod. Vertuo capsules use a barcode system the machine reads to set brew parameters, so third-party options are limited by design. If you want the freedom to buy whatever capsules are on the shelf, this isn't the machine for that. The feature list mentions over 40 permanent blends plus limited editions, so variety isn't the issue. Availability and cost per cup are, and neither is in the data. Capsule pricing varies by blend and subscription, and I won't guess at it here. Several specs you'd normally want are absent. There's no weight. No dimensions, no water reservoir capacity, no wattage, and no warranty information in the provided data. Water reservoir size in particular matters for a machine that brews 18 oz cups; if you're filling it daily. That's a real part of ownership. I can't tell you how large it is because it isn't listed. Summary The Vertuo Lattissima suits someone who wants espresso and milk drinks from one countertop unit without a separate frother, and who is fine buying Vertuo capsules exclusively. The six brew sizes and 30-second heat-up cover the practical morning routine, and the dishwasher-safe 16 fl, oz milk tank keeps cleanup simple. The main things to weigh are the capsule lock-in and the missing specs. Particularly reservoir capacity and physical dimensions. Who this is for This fits a household of one or two people who drink a mix of large coffees and milk-based drinks, and who value speed over tinkering. If you're the person who wants a latte before work without dirtying a pitcher and a frother, the one-touch milk system is the reason to pick this over a plain Vertuo machine. It also suits a small kitchen where a separate espresso machine and milk frother would eat counter space you don't have. Who should look elsewhere Skip it if you want to use third-party or refillable capsules, since Vertuo compatibility is exclusive. Skip it if you already own a standalone frother you like. Because you'd be paying for a milk system you won't use. And if you need to know the water reservoir capacity or exact footprint before buying, the provided data won't answer that, so factor in a trip to see it in person or check the manufacturer's full spec sheet.
