The one-touch control system The rotary switch does exactly two things: cook and keep warm. You turn it to COOK. And when the cycle finishes it drops into WARM automatically. No timer, no delay start, no porridge or brown rice presets. The listing says it handles white rice. Brown rice, oatmeal, porridge, vegetables, hot pot, and soup, but with a single mechanical cook cycle, the heavier items - brown rice especially - will need more time and possibly a second pass. That's not a defect. It's how this class of cooker works. A simple switch-based machine can't distinguish between a light white rice cycle and a dense brown rice cycle. Set your expectations there. In practice. What the one-touch design does buy you is reliability. Fewer components means fewer things to fail. And the warm function holds rice at serving temperature until you get to it. For someone who wants rice ready when they walk in and doesn't want to think about settings, this is the whole point. For everyday listening, the inner pot is the actual aspect The removable inner pot is the differentiator. It comes out. Works as a bowl, and gets washed directly - you skip the extra dish. That's a petite thing that adds up if you eat rice most days. The non-stick coating and the no-BPA. No-PFAS, no-PFOA claim cover the food-contact surfaces. The glass lid lets you watch the cook without lifting the cover and losing heat. Useful for porridge and soup. Where you want to catch a boil-over before it happens.
