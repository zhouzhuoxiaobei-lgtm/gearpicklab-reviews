The Seekins Precision HIT Precision 35mm 0 MOA LRF Mount Kit is aimed squarely at that setup. It bundles three things: a 35mm scope mount, a diving board for accessories, and an adjustable bubble level. The 35mm tube diameter matters because it narrows your scope options. If your optic uses a 30mm or 34mm main tube. This specific kit won't work - Seekins does offer those sizes separately, but the version reviewed here's 35mm only. we noticed 7075 aluminum is the headline material here. It's a zinc-alloyed grade that sits well above the 6061 aluminum used in plenty of mounts at this level. The practical difference shows up as resistance to flex under recoil and better thread durability where the ring caps and rail clamp bolt down. If you're running a magnum cartridge or a heavy precision rifle. That extra stiffness matters more than it does on a lightweight hunting build. The S-LOCK wedge design is Seekins' answer to a common failure point: the mount shifting on the rail. Instead of a simple crossbolt clamping against the picatinny slot, the wedge locks the mount into the rail recess. For a rifle that sees repeated transport in a case or a truck, that reduces the chance of your zero wandering between range sessions. It's a mechanical answer. Not a coating or a torque spec. The diving board is the capability that separates this kit from a standard precision mount. It puts a picatinny section forward of the front ring, so you can bolt a laser rangefinder or a micro red dot directly to the scope body. For a hunter or competitor who wants a WMLRF or a backup sight at 12 o'clock. That's a cleaner solution than stacking adapters. The tradeoff is weight and bulk forward of the turret - a diving board adds material where it's most noticeable in handling. For everyday listening, the adjustable bubble level addresses a specific concern: rifle cant. A few degrees of tilt at 800 yards translates to a miss. And the level gives you a visual reference without taking your eye off the target. It's adjustable, which means you can true it to your reticle rather than trusting the factory setting. That adjustment is worth doing carefully - a level that's off by a degree is worse than no level at all. Where the data runs thin: there's no weight listed for the kit. That's a meaningful omission for anyone counting ounces on a backcountry build. There's also no torque specification in the provided data. No ring height, and no centerline height above the rail. Ring height is a critical number for scope mounting - it determines whether your objective bell clears the barrel and how your cheek weld lines up. You'd need to find that from the manufacturer or a retailer's listing before committing. There's also no stated warranty or country of origin in the specs provided. For a mount at this material grade and aspect set, those are details worth confirming elsewhere. This kit suits a specific shooter: someone running a 35mm-tube precision scope who wants a rangefinder or red dot mounted to the scope rather than the handguard. And who values a level integrated into the mount. If you're building a long-range hunting rifle or a PRS-style rig and you've already settled on a 35mm optic, the bundled accessories save you from sourcing three separate parts. Look elsewhere if your scope uses a 30mm or 34mm tube. Or if you're weight-conscious and don't need the diving board or level. A bare mount without the accessories will be lighter and less investment-grade. If you're not sure about your ring height requirement, resolve that before buying anything - this kit doesn't publish it.
