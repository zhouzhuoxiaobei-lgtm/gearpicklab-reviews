 What the material gets you: 304 stainless is the standard for milk pitchers because it resists the acids and heat cycling that come with daily steaming, and it won't hold onto the smell of yesterday's milk the way some softer finishes can. The listing calls it stain-proof and rust-proof. Which is aspirational language rather than a tested claim, but the base material is the right one for the job. If you leave milk sitting in any stainless pitcher overnight, it will stain. Rinse it when you're done. What the spout gets you: A round spout is a real design choice, not decoration. Sharp spouts pour fast and are harder to feather at low flow. A round spout tends to give you a slower, more controllable stream, which is exactly what you want when you're drawing a stem or finishing a heart. The listing claims it's dripless, which is a strong word for any pitcher. In practice, the round profile reduces the drip that collects on the lip after a pour. Whether it eliminates it depends on how full you fill it and how fast you tip it. Capacity is the decision point. If it's genuinely 20 ounces, this is a general-purpose pitcher that works for a single cortado up to a two-cup morning. If it's 12, it's a one-drink-at-a-time tool that many home users will find cramped once they factor in the milk you need for a 12-ounce latte. For this class of milk frothing pitcher, capacity is the spec people get wrong most often when buying online. Limitations and data gaps The listing never states the pitcher's weight. Wall thickness, or handle construction. Wall thickness matters because thin stainless dents at the rim. And a dented rim ruins the spout's pour geometry. Without that number, there's no way to judge how it will hold up to being knocked against a counter or dropped in a sink. The listing also doesn't say whether the handle is welded or riveted, or whether the interior has volume markings. Markings are genuinely useful if you're trying to steam a consistent amount of milk, and their absence is a real convenience gap, not a dealbreaker. The yellow finish is the other unknown. Colored stainless usually means a coating or a painted exterior, and the listing doesn't specify which or how it's applied. Coatings can chip at the rim and base where the pitcher takes the most contact. The listing offers no information on this. So treat the color as cosmetic with an unstated durability profile.
