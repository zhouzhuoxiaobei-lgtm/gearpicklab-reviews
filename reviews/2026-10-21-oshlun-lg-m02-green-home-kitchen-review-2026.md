On a miter saw that's genuinely useful - you don't re-zero a laser after every bevel change the way you might with a housing-mounted unit. The tradeoff is that "fits most saws" only holds if your saw's arbor and flange dimensions match. A 12-inch saw and a 7-1/4-inch portable saw don't share flange geometry. So the wide size range in the specs is about coverage across models, not a universal fit. Check your specific saw's flange before you commit. Automatic activation via a centrifugal switch means the laser fires when the blade spins and shuts off when it stops. No separate button to forget, and no battery drain sitting idle in the shop. The batteries come pre-installed with a spare set in the box, which is a space-saving thing but saves a trip mid-project. The line sits just left of the kerf, so you align the edge of the beam with your cut mark rather than centering the line on it. That's a deliberate offset - it keeps the blade from obscuring the beam as it descends. For everyday listening. The recessed laser housing is the other practical detail: it protects the emitter from sawdust and the occasional bump when the saw is folded down or transported. Who should look elsewhere If you need a confirmed fit for an oddball saw. Or you want published accuracy and runtime numbers before buying, this doesn't give you enough to go on.
