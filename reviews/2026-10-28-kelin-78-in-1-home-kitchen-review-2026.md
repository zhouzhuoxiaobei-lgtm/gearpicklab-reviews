Whether it actually does depends on how you read the specs. The headline number is the ratchet: 58 teeth with a claimed 50Nm torque output. Tooth count is what determines how fine the swing arc is. For everyday listening, the 50Nm torque figure is the manufacturer's claim, and it's the number you'd want to verify in hand - it's a lot for a bit-driver format, and S2 steel is the material that would need to hold up to it.  The bit count breaks down in a way that tells you what this kit is actually for. Thirty-five of the 78 pieces are 28mm precision bits - that's the bulk of the set, and it signals the kit leans toward compact electronics and fine work rather than heavy construction. The 29 short 25mm bits cover the standard furniture and appliance fasteners. Only six bits are 45mm long, which is the count that limits you on recessed screws; if you regularly deal with deep-set bolts, six long bits is thin coverage and you may find yourself short. S2 steel is the right call for bits that see torque. In real-world use, it's a shock-resistant tool steel, and the black phosphate coating is there to fight corrosion. The long bits get an additional mercury chrome coating, which the data ties specifically to rust resistance on those longer pieces. That's a sensible place to spend the extra treatment, since long bits are the ones that flex and see more stress. The magnetic system has two parts worth noting. The handle holds bits magnetically, and the case has a recharging slot that restores magnetism to the bits over time. Color-coded rings on the bits speed up identification, which matters more than it sounds when you're swapping between 78 pieces mid-repair. For most people, the kit also throws in tweezers, a spudger, a magnetic mat, and a pickup tool - the precision-repair extras that separate a kit meant for electronics from one that's purely for furniture. Limitations and data gaps The spec sheet is silent on a few things you'd want before committing. There's also no bit-type list. On the practical side, the data tells you how many bits of each length, but not which head types (Phillips, Torx, hex, security bits) are included. That's the single biggest gap, because a 78-piece kit is only useful if the bit types match the fasteners you actually encounter. Summary This is a precision-leaning bit-driver kit built around a fine-tooth ratchet and S2 steel bits, with a strong emphasis on electronics work given the 35 precision bits and included tweezers and spudger. Buyers who need heavy fastening or deep-recess access should weigh whether the bit mix fits their jobs before choosing it. Who this is for This suits someone doing mixed light repair - replacing a laptop's internal screws, tightening cabinet hinges, fixing a compact appliance - who wants one organized case instead of a drawer of loose drivers. If you tinker with electronics and also handle occasional furniture assembly, the precision-bit majority plus the standard short bits covers both without buying two kits. The included spudger and tweezers make it more useful for prying open plastic housings than a plain screwdriver set would be. Who should look elsewhere If your work is mostly heavy fastening - deck screws, engine bolts, structural hardware - a bit-driver kit with precision bits as its core isn't the right tool, and the six long bits won't reach deep recesses. ### Frequently Asked Questions About the KELIN 78 IN 1
### How many bits come in the set, and what sizes are they?
The set has 70 bits total: 29 short bits at 25mm, 6 long bits at 45mm, and 35 precision bits at 28mm. The remaining pieces in the 78-piece count are the magnetic pickup tool, magnetic mat, tweezers, spudger, and 4 magnetic handle sections.

### What material are the bits made of, and will they rust?
The bits are hardened S2 alloy steel with a black phosphate coating for corrosion resistance. The long bits get an additional mercury chrome coating for extra rust protection.

### How much torque does the ratchet handle, and how many teeth does it have?
The ratchet is rated for 50Nm of torque with a 58-tooth mechanism. KELIN claims this saves about 80% effort in screwdriving compared to a standard driver.

### Does the case keep the bits magnetized?
Yes, the case includes a magnetic recharging slot that restores magnetism to the bits. The handle itself is magnetic and holds bits securely in place.

### What extra tools are included besides the bits and handle?
The set adds tweezers, a spudger, a magnetic pickup tool, and a magnetic mat, which cover precision electronics work and small component handling. Everything stores in a compact case.
