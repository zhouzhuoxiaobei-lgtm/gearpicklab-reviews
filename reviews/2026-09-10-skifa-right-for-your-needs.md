 The 25-pound per-mount figure is the number that matters most. A pair of adult all-mountain skis with bindings typically lands in the 12-to-18-pound range, so a single mount handles one pair comfortably. A snowboard with bindings usually falls between 8 and 15 pounds* (based on available data) depending on length and binding weight. Which means one mount per board is fine. The rubber contact points serve two purposes: they grip the ski or board surface and they prevent the edge from digging into a hard plastic or metal hook. For anyone storing race skis or a board with a fresh tune, that rubber interface is the difference between a clean edge and a gouged one. The automatic locking mechanism engages when you push the arm up - a spring-loaded cam holds the arm in place rather than relying on friction alone. That's a more reliable hold than gravity-based hooks, especially in a garage where temperature swings can loosen things over time among ski storage racks. Who this is for This rack fits a homeowner with a standard stud-framed garage who stores four to six pairs of skis or a couple of snowboards and wants them off the floor. If you've got a family of four with two pairs each and you're tired of tripping over gear in the dark, six mounts at 25 pounds each covers that load with margin. It also works for a ski lease cabin where you need quick grab-and-go access without hauling equipment inside. Who should look elsewhere If you're mounting to concrete block or brick, the included drywall screws won't cut it, and the data doesn't cover masonry anchors. If you store competition skis with razor edges and need a mount with a published rubber hardness spec to guarantee no base damage, the missing durometer figure is a real gap. And if you need to hang a 40-pound fat bike or a heavy ladder, 25 pounds per mount is the ceiling unless you add your own straps - at which point you're modifying the product to do a job it wasn't specced for.
