Rattan bannetons wick moisture away from the dough surface during the final proof, which helps the crust set up before the loaf hits the oven. That's what the claim about "oven spring" is pointing at - a drier outer layer means the score opens more cleanly and the loaf rises upward instead of spreading. The round basket handles your standard boule. The oval basket gives you a batard. Which slices into more uniform pieces and fits a narrower oven. If you bake one shape regularly. The second basket is the one that sits on the shelf. For everyday listening, the cloth liners are removable and washable, which matters more than it sounds. Bare rattan needs a heavy dusting of rice flour or it will grab wet dough. And cleaning it means brushing out dried flour without soaking the wood. With liners. You can run a wet dough straight from the fridge and throw the liner in the wash afterward. The tradeoff is that the liners soften the basket's spiral pattern. So loaves come out with less of that ridged look. The lame is a single-blade cutter with a cover. For scoring, that's all you need - one sharp blade and a steady hand. In real-world use. The cover keeps it from slicing whatever's in the drawer, which is a real concern for anyone who's nicked a finger reaching past an unprotected lame. Then there's the filler. Ten disposable bread bags. Fifteen stickers, four stencils, a flour duster, two brushes, and three different scraping or stirring tools. If you're gifting this to someone starting out.
