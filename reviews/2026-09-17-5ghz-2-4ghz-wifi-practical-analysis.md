 The probe count is the headline. Five meat probes plus one ambient probe means you can monitor different cuts, different thicknesses, or different doneness targets simultaneously. The ambient probe reads oven or smoker temperature through the "OVEN" port, using the included clamps. For anyone who's tried to juggle two or three single-probe thermometers during a cook, this eliminates that problem entirely. Each probe is color-coded, so you can track which reading belongs to which piece of meat without guessing. For comparison, accuracy is listed at 0.9°F, which is tight enough for low-and-slow cooking where a 5-degree swing matters. The calibration range of ±12.6°F lets you correct a probe that's drifted. Though you'd need a known reference to calibrate against. The temperature range covers everything from a freezer (-22°F) to a screaming-hot grill (572°F), so there's no scenario in normal cooking this won't handle. The 2500mAh battery is rechargeable via [USB-C](https://www.usb.org/usb-type-c), and Inkbird rates it at 40 hours minimum after a 3.5-hour charge. That's a full overnight brisket cook plus change. But "at least 40 hours" is the only battery figure given - no standby time, no low-battery warning threshold, no indication of how WiFi versus Bluetooth affects drain. WiFi radios draw more power than Bluetooth. So if you're relying on the WiFi connection for remote monitoring, expect the real-world number to land closer to the 40-hour floor than above it. The app handles real-time monitoring, history graphs you can download, and 29 USDA presets (the spec text says 23, the description says 29 - a minor inconsistency). The backlit LCD adjusts brightness through the app, which is useful when you're reading it in direct sun. The magnetic back and foldable stand give you placement options on a grill lid or a metal prep table.
